I am a PhD Candidate in Sociology at UC Berkeley. My research contributes to economic sociology, organizational theory, strategy, and climate politics. I focus on corporate sustainability as a lens into broader strategic, technical, and relational dynamics of organizational change under institutional pressures. I combine qualitative methods that yield close organizational insights with computational and survey approaches that reveal patterns across firms.

My dissertation investigates the emergence, adoption, and implementation of corporate climate disclosure. Climate disclosure—the collection and reporting of climate metrics to investors and regulators—has become a cornerstone of corporate climate governance today and is mandated across major financial markets worldwide. I analyze disclosure data from more than 6,000 global companies, over 140 interviews, 15 months of participant observation, and regulatory filings. Across four articles in the dissertation, I examine the financial interests that shape what gets measured and reported for climate accountability, how sustainability professionals inside companies coordinate across different sub-units to collect data for disclosure, how organizations navigate uncertainty in emissions measurement, and the extent to which reporting can lead to meaningful improvements in sustainability.

My work is published in Social Forces, Wiley Interdisciplinary Reviews: Climate Change, Research in the Sociology of Organizations, and Technology in Society. Prior to graduate school, I earned a B.S. in Computer Science with honors in Science, Technology, and Society, and an M.A. in Sociology from Stanford University.
